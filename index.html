03 / Progression

How to actually build definition

Weeks 1–2

Learn form and establish a baseline. Use two sets if necessary. Write down weights and repetitions.

Weeks 3–6

Work toward the top of each rep range. Increase resistance when you can complete all sets cleanly.

Weeks 7–10

Progress gradually. If your dumbbells are too light for rows or squats, consider adjustable weights or harder variations.

Weeks 11–12

Compare reps, running comfort, energy, and optionally photos in consistent lighting. Adjust one variable at a time.

Visible abs require developed abdominal muscles and individual fat distribution. Crunches don't selectively remove belly fat; strength training and adequate nutrition are the focus here.

06 / Important notes

Keep it sustainable

Visible muscle definition varies by genetics, lighting and natural fat distribution. You don't need to replicate the exact leanness of a reference photo to make meaningful progress. Your recent weight of 139.4 lb is lower than your prior recorded 145 lb; if weight continues to fall without intending it, or you notice low energy or poor recovery, increase intake and speak with a healthcare professional.

Chest fullness cannot be diagnosed from a photo. You described a longstanding vein-like bump beneath one nipple; mention it at your next routine exam. Seek earlier assessment if it changes, grows, becomes hard/fixed, or is associated with discharge or skin changes.

This plan is general fitness guidance. Adjust for injuries, medical conditions, pain, or professional advice.
